## [PHK Reflects on Open Source, Bikeshedding, and Reversible Decisions](https://queue.acm.org/detail.cfm?id=3818307) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Poul-Henning Kamp (PHK), a prominent FreeBSD developer, published a retrospective in ACM Queue reflecting on decades of open source contributions, including the origin of the 'bikeshedding' concept and the importance of reversible decisions. This article offers timeless wisdom for software engineering teams, emphasizing that trivial issues often consume disproportionate attention (bikeshedding) and that reversible decisions should be made quickly to avoid wasted effort. PHK coined the term 'bikeshedding' in 1999 based on Parkinson's Law of Triviality, and he advocates for treating reversible decisions with speed and instinct rather than prolonged debate.

hackernews · Ygg2 · Jul 18, 17:27 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48960155)

**Background**: Bikeshedding refers to the tendency to spend excessive time on minor, easy-to-understand issues while neglecting more complex, important ones. The concept originated from C. Northcote Parkinson's 1957 example of a committee debating a bicycle shed while approving a nuclear power plant design. Reversible decisions are those that can be undone at low cost, and frameworks like Jeff Bezos's 'Type 1 vs Type 2' decisions suggest speeding them up.

## [Regressive JPEGs: Images That Get Worse Over Time](https://maurycyz.com/projects/bad_jpeg/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Maurycy Z. has created a technique called 'regressive JPEGs' that reverses the typical progressive JPEG behavior, causing images to display progressively worse quality as they load. This is achieved by constructing JPEG files where later scans overwrite already rendered image data with lower quality versions. This creative hack demonstrates a novel manipulation of the JPEG format, sparking community interest in steganography, timing control, and unconventional web applications. While not practically useful, it highlights the flexibility of image compression standards and inspires further exploration of format quirks. The technique exploits the fact that each scan in a JPEG file explicitly sets its spectral range, allowing later scans to overwrite earlier data. No JavaScript is required, and it works with plain HTTP and standard JPEG decoding.

hackernews · vitaut · Jul 18, 03:14 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48954851)

**Background**: JPEG is a lossy image compression standard that supports progressive encoding, where low-frequency components are sent first to display a low-resolution preview. In a normal progressive JPEG, the image quality improves as more data is received. Regressive JPEGs invert this process by sending higher quality data first, then overwriting it with lower quality data in later scans.
