It could accelerate adoption of the Durable Objects pattern across the broader ecosystem. Celld aims to bring this pattern to a self-hosted environment, leveraging SQLite for local storage and S3-compatible storage for replication and durability.

## [Webhooks' Reliability Issues and the SCROLL Protocol Proposal](https://weli.dev/blog/the-valley-of-webhooks/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A blog post critically analyzes webhook limitations for state synchronization and proposes a new protocol called SCROLL (Synchronized Change Replication Over Line Logs), which is presented as an IETF-style draft. The post has gained significant community attention, with 136 points and 61 comments. This discussion highlights the growing need for reliable state synchronization in API design, as webhooks often fail to guarantee delivery and ordering. The proposed SCROLL protocol, which resembles an actual IETF draft (Braid-HTTP Subscriptions), could influence future standards and improve how systems sync data. The SCROLL protocol uses a GET request with a 'Prefer: stream' header to establish a subscription, similar to the Braid-HTTP Subscriptions draft. Community members point out potential inefficiencies, such as persistent connections for low-frequency events, and suggest alternatives like cursor pagination or hybrid approaches.

hackernews · weli · Aug 5, 15:22 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49184216)

**Background**: Webhooks are a common way for servers to notify clients of changes, but they suffer from issues like lack of delivery guarantees, ordering, and deduplication. State synchronization between systems often requires reliable event delivery, which webhooks may not provide. The SCROLL protocol aims to address these issues by proposing a standardized approach, drawing inspiration from IETF drafts like Braid-HTTP Subscriptions.
