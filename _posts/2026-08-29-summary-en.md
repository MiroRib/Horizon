## [DHS Uses Obscure Law to Secretly Obtain Journalists' Records](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/aug/29/trump-dhs-1509-summons-records-journalists-nonprofits) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The Trump administration's Department of Homeland Security (DHS) has been using an obscure legal provision, known as a 1509 summons, to secretly obtain private records from journalists, non-profits, and unions without judicial oversight. In one case, DHS obtained six months of phone records for Minneapolis journalist Georgia Fort from T-Mobile, including over 10,000 calls and texts, without notifying her until mid-July. This raises serious civil liberties concerns, as the government is bypassing traditional judicial oversight to access sensitive information about journalists and advocacy groups, potentially chilling press freedom and dissent. The practice could set a dangerous precedent for government surveillance without checks and balances. In several cases, DHS has withdrawn a 1509 summons after it was challenged in court, possibly to avoid a judicial ruling on its legality. Notably, T-Mobile complied with the request, while Google resisted, and DHS must go to court to enforce compliance if a company refuses.

hackernews · firefax · Aug 29, 18:44 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49492219)

**Background**: A 1509 summons is a type of administrative subpoena that DHS can issue without a judge's approval, compelling companies to produce records. Unlike a warrant, it does not require probable cause or judicial oversight, making it a powerful tool for surveillance. The law has been criticized for lacking transparency and accountability, as targets are often not notified until after records are obtained.

## [Samsung's Processing-in-Memory Faces Adoption Hurdles](https://chipsandcheese.com/p/hot-chips-2026-samsungs-processing) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Samsung presented its Processing-in-Memory (PIM) technology at Hot Chips 2026, aiming to reduce data movement by integrating compute into memory. The presentation highlighted potential benefits for AI and data-intensive workloads but also drew skepticism about practical constraints and historical adoption challenges. This technology could address the von Neumann bottleneck, which limits performance due to data transfer between CPU and memory. If successful, it could significantly improve energy efficiency and speed for AI and big data applications, potentially reshaping computing architectures. PIM can be implemented at various levels, such as within the memory cell, at the sense amplifier, or using simple cores near memory banks. Different memory technologies like DRAM, HBM, and ReRAM can be used, each adding design complexity.

hackernews · ingve · Aug 29, 06:06 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49487341)

**Background**: The von Neumann architecture separates memory and processing, causing a bottleneck when data is transferred between them. Non-von-Neumann architectures, including processing-in-memory, aim to overcome this by performing computation where data resides. PIM has been explored for decades but has seen limited commercial adoption due to programming challenges and specialized hardware constraints.
