## [Anthropic Formalizes Fermat's Last Theorem in Lean](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formalizing-fermats-last-theorem) ⭐️ 10.0/10

Anthropic has successfully formalized Fermat's Last Theorem in the Lean proof assistant, a milestone in AI-driven mathematics. The project produced 13 million lines of Lean code and proved 29,500 intermediate theorems. This demonstrates that AI can now formalize large, complex mathematical proofs, potentially catching errors in existing proofs and easing the burden of refereeing new work. It marks a significant step toward AI-assisted mathematical verification and could reshape how mathematics is validated and communicated. The formalization follows the Darmon–Diamond–Taylor exposition (1995) of the Wiles–Taylor–Wiles argument, not the modern proof. The repository develops Fontaine theory and Mazur's work on the Eisenstein ideal to conclude that no Frey curve can have a point of order p.

**Background**: Fermat's Last Theorem, stated by Pierre de Fermat in 1637, claims no three positive integers a, b, c satisfy a^n + b^n = c^n for n > 2. It was proven by Andrew Wiles in 1994 using advanced algebraic geometry and number theory. Lean is an interactive theorem prover that allows formalizing mathematical statements and proofs in a machine-checkable way.
