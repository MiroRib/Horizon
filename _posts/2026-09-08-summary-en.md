This provides practical guidance for deploying large language models on limited hardware, helping users choose quantization levels that balance quality and resource usage. It also highlights the limits of extreme quantization for this model family. The benchmark uses Wilson 95% confidence intervals to indicate noise, and notes that differences are minimal down to 4-bit, with 2-bit scoring slightly lower. ## [Chipmakers Adopt ASML's $400M High-NA EUV Machines, Back 12-Inch Photomasks](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/09/top-chipmakers-embrace-asmls-400m-machines-agree-to-crucial-chipmaking-change/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Top chipmakers, including TSMC, Samsung, and Intel, are adopting ASML's high-NA EUV lithography machines, which cost around $400 million each. They have also agreed to transition from the 40-year-old 6-inch photomask standard to a new 12-inch format to boost productivity by up to 40%. This shift is significant because high-NA EUV is essential for producing next-generation chips at smaller nodes, and the move to 12-inch photomasks addresses a key limitation that caused throughput loss. The adoption could accelerate advanced chip production and reduce costs, impacting the entire semiconductor industry. The high-NA EUV machines use a numerical aperture of 0.55, compared to 0.33 in previous EUV systems, enabling finer patterning. However, the smaller exposure field of high-NA EUV caused a 30% throughput loss, which the 12-inch photomask standard aims to recover by allowing larger patterns to be printed in a single exposure.

rss · Ars Technica · Sep 8, 19:12

**Background**: EUV (extreme ultraviolet) lithography is a critical process in chipmaking that uses light with a wavelength of 13.5 nm to pattern circuits on silicon wafers. High-NA (high numerical aperture) EUV is the latest generation, offering higher resolution but requiring larger photomasks to maintain productivity. Photomasks are templates that contain the circuit patterns; transitioning from 6-inch to 12-inch masks is a major industry change.
