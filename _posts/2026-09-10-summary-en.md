## [OpenAI's Navier-Stokes result ships with a Lean 4 formal proof](https://www.johndcook.com/blog/2026/09/09/formal-method-revolution/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

On 8 September 2026, OpenAI claimed a counter-example showing breakdown of Navier-Stokes solutions in three-dimensional Euclidean space, and released it together with a formalization in the Lean 4 proof assistant. The proof was produced by a swarm of roughly 10,000 AI agents running an internal frontier model, and OpenAI stated it would not claim the $1 million Clay Millennium Prize for the result. This is a milestone for automated theorem proving: an AI system produced a machine-checkable formal proof for a result tied to one of the Clay Millennium Prize Problems, suggesting AI can now contribute to research-level mathematics rather than only routine lemmas. It also fuels debate about verification costs, priority, and whether human mathematicians can independently check AI-generated arguments. The counter-example has not yet been verified by external mathematicians or the Clay Mathematics Institute, and the announcement was accompanied by a priority dispute with Levent Alpöge (then at Anthropic) and Tristan Buckmaster, who had derived closely related results on the Euler equations. The method builds on a 2023 technique by Diego Córdoba and Luis Martínez-Zoroa for finding blowup phenomena in related fluid equations.

**Background**: Lean is a proof assistant and functional programming language based on the Calculus of Inductive Constructions; Lean 4, released in 2021, compiles to C and supports efficient domain-specific automation. The Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem asks whether smooth solutions always exist in three dimensions, and was named a Millennium Prize Problem in 2000. Automated theorem proving is the subfield of automated reasoning concerned with proving mathematical theorems by computer programs.

Forgejo released versions 16.0.4 and 15.0.8 to fix a critical remote code execution (RCE) vulnerability (CVE-2026-89094) affecting 16.0.3 and earlier, where template expansion during repository initialization could be exploited via a crafted template repository. The fix prevents template expansion from interfering with git repo initialization. This is a high-impact security event for a widely used self-hosted Git service, as RCE allows attackers to execute arbitrary code on the server, potentially compromising all hosted repositories and user data. ## [AI Data Centers' Power Demands Threaten Grid Stability](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/09/10/1141649/powering-ai-is-an-architecture-problem/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

An MIT Technology Review article reports that on July 22, 2026, a transmission line fault in Ashburn, Virginia—the world's largest data center cluster—knocked over 3 gigawatts of load off the grid in seconds, following a similar 2024 event where a single failed surge arrester dropped roughly 1,500 megawatts across 60 Virginia facilities. The article argues these large-scale load loss events reveal a systemic grid stability risk driven by AI data centers' concentrated, power-electronics-based loads, and calls for architectural solutions rather than incremental fixes. As AI training and inference scale up, data centers are becoming gigawatt-scale loads whose sudden disconnection can destabilize regional grids, affecting millions of electricity customers beyond the data center operators themselves. This shifts the conversation from energy efficiency to grid resilience, meaning AI infrastructure planning must now account for dynamic stability, protection coordination, and load-shedding behavior as first-class design constraints. The incidents involved power-electronics-interfaced loads, which can disconnect or change consumption far faster than traditional mechanical loads, and the 3 GW event was triggered by a single transmission line fault rather than a generation failure. Industry reports such as ESIG's analysis of large load disturbance events point to systemic gaps in how grid planners model and mitigate these fast, concentrated load swings.

rss · MIT Technology Review · Sep 10, 11:00

**Background**: A surge arrester is a protective device that limits voltage on grid equipment by shunting surge current to ground in microseconds, preventing insulation damage. Modern data centers use large amounts of power electronics—such as uninterruptible power supplies and variable-frequency drives—which behave differently from traditional motors and can drop load almost instantaneously. Grid operators have historically planned around gradual load changes, so concentrated gigawatt-scale loads that trip in seconds create new stability challenges that existing protection and planning frameworks were not designed for.

## [NASA's Decorrelation Stretch Reveals Ancient Rock Art](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/Manipulating_Satellite_Photos_Now_Reveals_Ancient_Images) ⭐️ 7.0/10

NASA's decorrelation stretch technique, originally developed for enhancing satellite and Mars imagery, is now being widely applied to reveal ancient rock art and other hidden details in photographs. The technique works by removing inter-channel color correlation to dramatically heighten subtle color contrasts that are invisible to the naked eye. This is a notable example of NASA technology transfer creating unexpected value in archaeology and cultural heritage preservation, allowing researchers to study faded rock art without invasive methods. It also demonstrates how fundamental signal processing techniques can find new life across completely different domains decades after their original development. Decorrelation stretch operates on three color channels simultaneously, transforming the image so its color planes become uncorrelated with assigned variances; however, the standard algorithm can suffer from numerical instability and cannot handle degenerate cases where color planes are linearly dependent. The technique is accessible to hobbyists and researchers alike, with implementations available in tools like GIMP and MATLAB.

hackernews · gumby · Sep 10, 15:29 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49645437)

**Background**: Decorrelation stretch is an image enhancement technique that emphasizes color differences by removing the correlation between color channels in an image. It was originally developed for remote sensing and planetary science, notably used by NASA to study Mars surface features. The technique is particularly effective on multispectral datasets, where subtle spectral differences carry important information that the human eye cannot easily distinguish.
