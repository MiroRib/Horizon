Researchers at Ledger Donjon demonstrated a photon-emission-guided laser fault injection attack that bypasses the RP2350 microcontroller's secure debug protections, allowing unauthorized access to its secure enclave. The attack requires physical access, destructive chip preparation, and approximately $250,000 in laboratory equipment. This attack highlights that even modern secure enclaves like the RP2350's can be physically compromised, which is critical for developers relying on it for security-sensitive applications such as hardware key storage. It underscores the ongoing arms race between hardware security designers and attackers, and may influence future secure chip designs. The attack uses photon emission analysis to locate the secure logic, then precisely targets it with a laser to induce faults that bypass debug access controls. The high cost and need for destructive preparation limit its practicality, but the technique could be adapted with cheaper equipment as demonstrated in community discussions.

Fault injection attacks deliberately introduce errors into a chip's operation to bypass security measures, and laser fault injection is a powerful physical attack that uses focused light to flip bits. Photon emission analysis observes light emitted by transistors to map chip activity, aiding in targeting the attack.

## [Cactus Needle 3: 8-29MB models match DeepSeek V4 Flash on tool calls](https://cactuscompute.com/needle) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Cactus released Needle 3, a family of automation-focused models that ship as 8-29MB 2-bit binaries (25M-121M parameters) and handle tool calls and structured JSON output rather than chat. The 20-layer model scores 86.0 on the Mobile Actions benchmark, beating LFM2.5 1.2B (82.4), Qwen3.5 0.8B (76.0), and Apple's on-device model (57.6). This shows that extremely small, task-specific models can rival much larger LLMs on narrow automation tasks, making on-device tool calling practical for low-power environments like cars, homes, and industrial PLCs. It also signals a shift toward fine-tuning tiny models for production rather than relying on large general-purpose models. Needle 3 uses a novel 'intelligence laddering' technique where every layer from 2 to 20 is a deployable subnetwork sharing one set of weights, plus a Monarch Hadamard MLP that replaces the dense FFN with O(d√d) parameters. It supports seven languages, calibrated confidence scores, regex-based triggers, and platforms from macOS and Linux to iOS, Android, WebAssembly, and RISC-V.

hackernews · HenryNdubuaku · Sep 18, 00:11 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49748553)

**Background**: Needle is a line of tiny models from Cactus designed specifically for automation — deciding which tool to call and emitting structured JSON — rather than general conversation, since packing broad chat ability into such small models is hard. 'Intelligence laddering' means a single trained network can be truncated at different depths to trade accuracy for size, while the Monarch Hadamard MLP is a structured matrix technique that cuts the parameter cost of the feed-forward layers. DeepSeek V4 Flash is a much larger frontier model used here as a performance reference point.
